Autonomous

Driverless software for Formula Student, built and tested in PicoSim

A full autonomous racing stack, from raw LiDAR and camera data to steering and pedals, run against the PicoSim vehicle model so it can be developed, broken and fixed long before it reaches a real car.

Pipeline

Perception

LiDAR point-cloud clustering with cone colour from return intensity, fused with a YOLO detector on the camera. The detector is trained on images rendered by PicoSim and fine-tuned on the public FSOCO dataset.

State estimation & SLAM

IMU and wheel-speed EKF for velocity, and EKF-SLAM for the cone map and the pose of the car, including gyro bias.

Planning

Delaunay triangulation of the cone map with triangle filters and a beam search along the track centre. Once the track is known, a minimum-curvature racing line between the cone boundaries.

Control

Lateral MPC with understeer compensation and actuator-delay prediction, and a speed profile from the measured G-G-V envelope of the car. A slip-angle based spin guard backs it up.

Missions

Acceleration, skidpad, autocross, trackdrive and EBS test, with the autonomous system counting its own laps and stopping after the finish line as the rules require.

Simulation side

Raw LiDAR models for several sensors, camera rendering, IMU and wheel-speed noise, actuator lag and EBS reaction, rule-compliant random tracks, and a regression suite that scores every run against the competition rules.

How it compares

Leading Formula Student teams publish much of their work, and their papers shaped many choices here. The table shows where this project differs. It is a simulation-only stack; the teams below race real cars.

FSDS
Formula Student Driverless Simulator		 Open simulator built on Unreal Engine and AirSim with a ROS bridge, used for virtual competitions. Vehicle dynamics are the core here rather than a generic car model, and missions are judged and scored with the competition rules after every run.
Commercial simulators Validated vehicle and sensor models with HIL support, under paid licenses. Free to change at every level and built around Formula Student missions. Not yet validated against a real car.

Strengths

  • One vehicle model for everything. The autonomy stack drives the same 1 kHz model with Magic Formula tires that a human driver feels through the force-feedback wheel.
  • Perception is in the loop. Raw LiDAR and camera data, not ready-made cone lists, so detection errors reach the planner the way they would on a car.
  • Real-car effects on purpose. Sensor noise, IMU bias, wheel-speed errors, steering lag, command delay and EBS reaction time can all be switched on to see where the stack breaks.
  • Rules built in. Every mission is judged like at competition, and a regression suite reruns them after each change.

What still needs work

  • Exploration lap speed. The first lap on an unknown track is still far slower than top teams and is the largest gap in points.
  • Sim-to-real for the camera. A detector trained only on rendered images does poorly on real photos; fine-tuning on FSOCO helps, but more real data and domain randomisation are needed.
  • High-channel LiDAR. Seeing many more cones currently confuses the planner; cone selection should follow the track boundaries rather than a radius.
  • Mapping. Top teams use graph-based SLAM; this stack uses EKF-SLAM and still keeps some false cones.
  • Latency. Above about 100 ms of command delay the stack stays safe only by slowing down.
  • Toward a real car. LiDAR reflectivity and incidence-angle dropout, camera distortion and exposure, ROS 2 integration and hardware-in-the-loop are next.

Roadmap

Goal: a driverless stack that is competitive in simulation first, then moves to the real car with as few surprises as possible.

  1. PHASE 1

    Foundation done

    • Raw LiDAR and camera simulation; cone perception with YOLO trained on rendered images
    • EKF-SLAM, Delaunay-based path planning, minimum-curvature racing line, lateral MPC
    • All competition missions with the autonomous system counting its own laps
    • Regression suite that scores every run with the competition rules
  2. PHASE 2

    Competitive driving in progress

    • Faster exploration lap on an unknown track
    • Cone selection that follows the track boundaries, so high-channel LiDAR helps instead of hurting
    • Graph-based SLAM and fewer false cones in the map
    • Stable at full speed with realistic command latency
  3. PHASE 3

    Real-sensor readiness planned

    • Camera detector trained on real images (FSOCO) with domain randomisation of the rendered data
    • LiDAR reflectivity and incidence-angle dropout; camera distortion and exposure
    • Sensor calibration workflow inside the simulator
  4. PHASE 4

    ROS 2 & hardware in the loop planned

    • Stack ported to ROS 2 with the simulator as a drop-in sensor source
    • Software- and hardware-in-the-loop runs on the target computer, CAN emulation
  5. PHASE 5

    On the car planned

    • Sensor mounting and calibration on the real vehicle
    • Staged vehicle tests from low-speed runs to full missions, compared against the simulator
